Privacy Policy

Effective May 16, 2026

This document explains exactly what data Poof accesses on your device, what it does with that data, and what (if anything) leaves your device. Poof is built on three commitments:

  • No ads. Ever.
  • No tracking. No analytics, no crash reporting, no device identifiers, no third-party SDKs.
  • No accounts. You don't sign in. There is no server holding your data because there is no server.

If at any point this policy contradicts what the app actually does, the app is wrong; please contact us so we can fix it.

The short version

Poof is a utility toolkit. Most tools work entirely on your device and never make a network request. Two tools (Speed Test and Currency Converter) make HTTP requests to Cloudflare and a public exchange-rate API respectively, and only when you actively use them. No request to any third party contains your contacts, files, location, notifications, or any user-identifying information.

When a tool needs access to something on your device (your camera, microphone, location, contacts, notifications, or installed apps), Android prompts you to grant permission, and you can revoke it at any time from Android Settings. Poof never accesses any of these without an explicit user action.

What we access, and where it goes

1. Location

Tools
Prayer Times, Sun & Moon (sunrise/sunset), Qibla.
Accessed
Your device's GPS coordinates (latitude/longitude) via Android's standard Location Manager. Both Approximate and Precise location may be requested; coarse coordinates are used as fallback.
Used for
Calculating prayer times, sunrise/sunset, and Qibla bearing locally on the device. Prayer Times stores your last-known coordinates in app-private storage so it can compute prayer times offline.
Goes to
Nowhere. Coordinates never leave your device. There is no Poof server.

All three tools accept manual city entry instead of GPS. Location permission can be revoked any time from Android Settings.

2. Camera

Tools
QR Generator/Scanner, Document Scanner, Magnifier, Camera Inspector, Image OCR, Live Color Picker, Authenticator (scanning a two-factor setup QR code), Loyalty Cards (scanning the barcode on a physical card).
Accessed
Camera frames in real time.
Used for
On-device processing using Google's ML Kit (the on-device variant: no Firebase ML, no Google Cloud Vision). Frames are analyzed in memory for barcodes, text, document edges, or color.
Goes to
Nowhere. No frames are saved. No frames are uploaded.

3. Microphone

Tools
Decibel Meter.
Accessed
Real-time audio amplitude (RMS) only.
Used for
Displaying the current sound level. The audio buffer is read, the loudness measured, and discarded.
Goes to
Nowhere. No audio is recorded or transmitted.

4. Contacts and call history

Tools
Call Log / Contacts tool, Birthdays tool (optional "Import from Contacts" action).
Accessed
Names, phone numbers, and call entries (incoming/outgoing/missed, date, duration) via Android's standard Contacts and Call Log providers. The Birthdays tool additionally reads the birthday field and the contact photo URI of contacts that have one.
Used for
Displaying contacts and call history on screen so you can browse them. In the Birthdays tool, the contacts you pick from the import dialog are saved as on-device birthday entries: name, date, and (if available) a copy of the contact's photo are written to the tool's local database / private files directory. Photos are downscaled and stored only on this device.
Goes to
Nowhere. Nothing is transmitted. The Call Log / Contacts tool clears its in-memory list when closed; Birthdays entries persist on this device until you delete them from the tool.

5. Notifications

Tool
Notification History.
Accessed
When (and only when) you enable Notification History and grant the Notification Listener permission, Poof receives a copy of every notification posted to your device, including the title, body text, and posting app.
Used for
Storing the captured notifications in an on-device database so you can search and review them later.
Goes to
Nowhere we control. Stored locally on your device.

You can:

  • Disable capture entirely from the tool's settings.
  • Exclude specific apps from capture.
  • Skip ongoing/persistent notifications.
  • Suppress text capture (keep titles only).
  • Auto-prune entries older than your chosen retention period.
  • Manually delete individual or all entries.
  • Export entries as a CSV (Pro feature), saved to your Downloads folder or shared via Android's share sheet to a destination you pick. Poof does not upload it anywhere on its own.

Notifications can contain sensitive information (one-time codes, message previews, banking alerts). Capture is opt-in and you control what is captured.

6. Installed apps and usage stats

Tool
App Inspector.
Accessed
The list of apps installed on your device, their permissions, install/update times, sizes, target SDK, and (if you grant Usage Access) their last-used dates.
Used for
Displaying the list so you can audit what's installed and what permissions each app declares.
Goes to
Nowhere. The list is never transmitted. You can optionally export visible apps as a CSV (Pro feature) saved to your Downloads folder.

7. Files and media you select

Tools
PDF tools (merge, split, compress, watermark, sign, redact, etc.), Image tools (compress, convert, redact, resize, watermark, EXIF viewer, etc.), OCR, Document Scanner.
Accessed
Only the specific files you pick using Android's system file picker. Poof does not browse your storage.
Used for
Processing on-device using local libraries (PDFBox, Android image APIs, on-device ML Kit). Outputs are saved to a destination you choose.
Goes to
Nowhere. No file is uploaded. Temporary working files in the app's cache are deleted when the operation finishes.

8. Sensors

Tools
Compass (rotation vector), Qibla (accelerometer + magnetometer), Bubble Level (accelerometer).
Accessed
Real-time sensor readings via Android's SensorManager.
Used for
Calculating orientation, heading, or tilt for live display.
Goes to
Nowhere. Readings are not stored or transmitted.

9. Clipboard

Tools
Several tools (Base64 encoder, base converter, unit converter, NFC writer, coordinate paste, etc.) include a "Paste from clipboard" button. The Universal Inspector also reads the clipboard once on app launch to detect shared text and route it to the right tool.
Used for
Showing the pasted content in the relevant tool's input field so you can act on it.
Goes to
Nowhere. Clipboard contents are not persisted unless you explicitly save them inside a tool (e.g. saving a generated QR code to QR History).

10. Personal dates (Birthdays tool)

Tool
Birthdays: track birthdays and anniversaries with reminders.
Accessed
Names, dates, optional notes, and optional photos that you add to the tool. Photos can be added either by picking one from your gallery using Android's system Photo Picker, or by importing birthdays from your Contacts (in which case the contact's existing photo is brought in alongside the birthday date).
Used for
Storing the entries you create in the tool's on-device database. Photos are downscaled to roughly 1024 px on the long edge and written to the app's private files directory so the tool keeps working even if the source image is later moved or deleted from your gallery. The same data drives the on-screen list, the home-screen widget, the optional reminder notifications, and the "Send a wish" share image you can generate from an entry.
Goes to
Nowhere. Entries and photos live only on this device and are deleted when you delete the entry (or use "Delete all"). Sharing a wish image goes through Android's standard share sheet to whichever app you pick. Poof does not upload anything on its own.
Optional lock
The tool offers an opt-in "Lock with biometric" toggle. When on, opening Birthdays requires biometric authentication (fingerprint / face) or, as a fallback, your device PIN / pattern / password. The biometric match happens entirely inside Android's secure subsystem. No biometric data ever reaches Poof. Only a single on/off preference is stored. The lock gates the screen; the underlying entries continue to live in the same on-device storage as before.

11. Journal (Diary tool)

Tool
Diary: private on-device journal with one entry per day, mood tracking, photos, calendar view, streaks, daily reminders, and an optional biometric lock.
Accessed
The text, mood selection, and photos you choose to attach to each daily entry. Photos are added by picking from your gallery using Android's system Photo Picker (up to 5 per entry). The tool reads your device's wall clock and time zone so the daily reminder fires at the time you configure.
Used for
Storing entries in the tool's on-device database. Attached photos are downscaled to roughly 1600 px on the long edge and written to the app's private files directory so they keep working even if the source is later moved or deleted from your gallery. The same data drives the in-app calendar, streak counter, mood chart, "On this day" memory card, the home-screen widget (today's mood + streak), and the optional daily reminder notification.
Goes to
Nowhere. Entries and photos live only on this device and are deleted when you remove the entry, use "Delete all", or uninstall the app. Sharing exports through Android's standard share sheet routes to whichever app you choose; Poof does not upload anything on its own.
Exports you can create
The tool offers two export formats via Android's system file picker (you choose the destination: local folder, Drive, email attachment, anywhere):
  • Markdown: one text file containing every entry's date, mood, and content. Photos are not included; the markdown is plain text.
  • PDF: a fully rendered document containing every entry's text and attached photos, paginated. Used for portable backups or printing.
Importing markdown back into the tool restores the text + mood entries; if you previously exported on a different device, any photo references that don't exist on this device are skipped (markdown carries paths only).
Optional lock
Same opt-in biometric lock as the Birthdays tool. Authentication is done entirely by Android's secure subsystem; no biometric data is exposed to Poof, and only an on/off preference is stored.

12. Authenticator (two-factor codes)

Tool
Authenticator: generates the rotating two-factor-authentication codes for your accounts.
Accessed
The secret keys for your 2FA accounts. You add a key either by scanning the setup QR code your provider shows (using the camera, processed on-device) or by typing the key in manually.
Used for
Storing each secret key in the tool's on-device database so the app can generate your codes. Every key is encrypted at rest using the Android Keystore (hardware-backed on devices that support it). Codes are computed locally from the key and the current time; the tool makes no network requests.
Goes to
Nowhere on its own. The secret keys never leave your device unless you choose to export a backup (below).
Backup & restore
The tool can export an encrypted backup of your accounts through Android's system file picker, to a destination you choose (local folder, Drive, email attachment, anywhere). The backup file is encrypted with a password you set; that password is never stored, and Poof cannot recover it or read the backup without it. Restoring reads such a file back. Poof does not upload or sync anything on its own.
Optional lock
Same opt-in biometric lock as the Birthdays and Diary tools. Authentication is handled entirely by Android's secure subsystem; no biometric data is exposed to Poof, and only an on/off preference is stored.

13. Loyalty Cards

Tool
Loyalty Cards: stores the barcodes from your supermarket, membership, gift, library, transit, and gym cards so you can show them at the till.
Accessed
For each card you add: the store name you give it, the card number printed on it, the barcode symbology (e.g. Code 128, EAN-13), an optional note, an optional card-type label (Loyalty, Gift, Membership, etc.), and the tile colour you pick. You add a card either by scanning its barcode with the camera (processed on-device by Google's ML Kit) or by typing the number in manually.
Used for
Storing each card in the tool's on-device database so the app can re-render its barcode for a checkout scanner. The barcode is generated locally each time; nothing is fetched from a logo or merchant database. The tool makes no network requests.
Goes to
Nowhere on its own. Card numbers never leave your device unless you choose to export a backup (below).
Backup & restore
The tool can export an encrypted backup of all your cards through Android's system file picker, to a destination you choose (local folder, Drive, email attachment, anywhere). The backup file is encrypted with a password you set; that password is never stored, and Poof cannot recover it or read the backup without it. Restoring reads such a file back. Poof does not upload or sync anything on its own.

14. NFC Tags

Tool
NFC Tags: read, write, erase, clone, and password-protect the small NFC stickers and tags you tap your phone against.
Accessed
The contents of a tag (URL, text, Wi-Fi credentials, contact details, etc.) when you tap one against the phone, plus the tag's unique chip identifier (UID). For the Protect feature, the 4-character password you type into the form.
Used for
Displaying what's on a scanned tag, writing the content you enter onto a tag, cloning one tag's contents onto another, and (on NTAG213 / 215 / 216 chips) setting or removing an NTAG21x password. A short list of recent scans (up to 20) and your saved Write templates are kept in on-device storage so you can find them again later. When you set a password on a tag, the password is stored on the device (keyed by the tag's UID) so subsequent writes to the same tag don't ask for it again; choosing "Remove" in Protect mode clears it from the device.
Goes to
Nowhere. All NFC traffic is between your phone and the physical tag in front of it. Scanned content, saved templates, recent-scan summaries, and remembered passwords all live only on this device, and the tool makes no network requests.

What leaves your device

Five tools, and only when you use them.

Speed Test → Cloudflare

When you run a speed test, the tool exchanges data with Cloudflare's public speed-test infrastructure:

  • https://speed.cloudflare.com/meta : returns your public IP, ISP name, and detected city
  • https://speed.cloudflare.com/__down : downloads test bytes for download-speed measurement
  • https://speed.cloudflare.com/__up : uploads random bytes for upload-speed measurement

Test results (Mbps, latency, jitter) are saved to a local on-device database so you can see your history. Nothing about your test results is sent to Poof or anywhere else. Cloudflare's privacy policy applies to the Cloudflare side: cloudflare.com/privacypolicy

Currency Converter → open.er-api.com

When you open the Currency Converter, it fetches current exchange rates from https://open.er-api.com/v6/latest/{currency}. The request contains only the base-currency code (e.g. USD). Rates are cached on your device so the converter works offline.

DNS Lookup → Cloudflare, Google

When you run a DNS lookup, the tool sends the hostname or IP you typed to one or more public DNS-over-HTTPS resolvers and shows you what they returned. The request contains only the hostname (or IP for reverse lookups) and the record type (A, AAAA, MX, etc.) you picked. The endpoints used are:

  • https://1.1.1.1/dns-query (Cloudflare)
  • https://dns.google/resolve (Google Public DNS)

In Compare mode the same request is sent in parallel to both so you can see how their answers differ. In Single mode only the resolver you chose is contacted. The tool can also resolve via the device's system DNS for A and AAAA records (your phone's normal resolver path, controlled by Android Settings or Private DNS), in which case the request goes wherever Android sends it. Each resolver's privacy policy applies to its side: Cloudflare, Google Public DNS.

URL Unshortener → whichever URL you paste

The URL Unshortener follows every redirect of a short URL you give it and shows the final destination plus a preview of that page's title and description. This is different from the tools above: it doesn't contact a fixed service. It contacts whatever URL you typed and whatever URL that one redirects to, all the way to the end of the chain (up to ten hops). Each request goes out from your device's normal IP, with a generic browser-style User-Agent and no cookies or identifiers.

By design, this means the destination server can see your IP making the request, in the same way it would if you tapped the link in any browser. That is the point of the tool: it visits the URL on your behalf so you can see where it goes before deciding to open it yourself. Nothing about the traced URL or the destination is stored after you leave the screen.

What's My IP → ipwho.is, icanhazip.com

When you open the What's My IP tool, it makes up to three requests, all in parallel:

  • https://ipwho.is/ : returns your public IP plus geolocation and network metadata (city, region, country, ISP, ASN, timezone). Whichever IP version your device's preferred stack chose comes back here.
  • https://ipv4.icanhazip.com : returns your IPv4 address as plain text, or fails silently if your network has no IPv4 path.
  • https://ipv6.icanhazip.com : returns your IPv6 address as plain text, or fails silently if your network has no IPv6 path.

Each service identifies you by the source IP of the request itself, the same way any website sees your IP. No request body, no API key, no account, no device identifier. The tool does not store the result; closing the screen discards it. icanhazip.com is operated by Cloudflare. The relevant privacy policies are: ipwho.is, Cloudflare.

No personally identifying information is sent to any of these services. All requests are made from your device's IP (same as any web browser) and contain no Poof-assigned user ID or device identifier (because none exists).

What we do not do

  • No analytics. No Firebase, Mixpanel, Amplitude, Segment, Sentry, Bugsnag, Crashlytics, AppsFlyer, Adjust, or anything similar.
  • No advertising. No AdMob, Unity Ads, AppLovin, IronSource, etc.
  • No accounts. No sign-in, no email collection, no profile.
  • No device identifiers. No GAID, ANDROID_ID, IMEI, MAC address.
  • No cloud backup. Auto-backup and device-transfer rules are disabled, so your Poof data does not go to Google Drive even if the rest of your phone does. The Authenticator and Loyalty Cards tools' optional backups are different: they are exports you trigger yourself, encrypted with your password, saved wherever you choose.
  • No data selling. There is no data to sell. There is no buyer.

Pro purchases

Poof Pro is a one-time, non-recurring in-app purchase. The transaction itself is handled by Google Play. Poof receives a confirmation that you own the unlock and stores a single boolean (is_pro = true) in app-private storage. We don't see your name, email, payment method, or transaction ID. Google Play's privacy policy governs the purchase: policies.google.com/privacy

Children

Poof is a general-purpose utility app. It is not directed at children. The app does not knowingly collect any data that could identify a child or anyone else.

Your controls

  • Revoke any permission from Android Settings → Apps → Poof → Permissions.
  • Delete any captured data from inside the relevant tool (notification history, QR history, speed-test history, etc.).
  • Clear all app data from Android Settings → Apps → Poof → Storage → Clear data. This wipes every preference, history entry, and cached file. Poof will be in its first-launch state.
  • Uninstall. Removing the app removes all of its data (Auto-Backup is disabled, so nothing is restored elsewhere).

Changes to this policy

If we change what the app does with data, this policy will be updated and the effective date at the top will change. Material changes will be highlighted in-app on first launch after the change.

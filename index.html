Privacy Policy

Effective May 9, 2026

This document explains exactly what data Poof accesses on your device, what it does with that data, and what (if anything) leaves your device. Poof is built on three commitments:

  • No ads. Ever.
  • No tracking. No analytics, no crash reporting, no device identifiers, no third-party SDKs.
  • No accounts. You don't sign in. There is no server holding your data because there is no server.

If at any point this policy contradicts what the app actually does, the app is wrong; please contact us so we can fix it.

The short version

Poof is a utility toolkit. Most tools work entirely on your device and never make a network request. Two tools (Speed Test and Currency Converter) make HTTP requests to Cloudflare and a public exchange-rate API respectively, and only when you actively use them. No request to any third party contains your contacts, files, location, notifications, or any user-identifying information.

When a tool needs access to something on your device (your camera, microphone, location, contacts, notifications, or installed apps), Android prompts you to grant permission, and you can revoke it at any time from Android Settings. Poof never accesses any of these without an explicit user action.

What we access, and where it goes

1. Location

Tools
Prayer Times, Sun & Moon (sunrise/sunset), Qibla.
Accessed
Your device's GPS coordinates (latitude/longitude) via Android's standard Location Manager. Both Approximate and Precise location may be requested; coarse coordinates are used as fallback.
Used for
Calculating prayer times, sunrise/sunset, and Qibla bearing locally on the device. Prayer Times stores your last-known coordinates in app-private storage so it can compute prayer times offline.
Goes to
Nowhere. Coordinates never leave your device. There is no Poof server.

All three tools accept manual city entry instead of GPS. Location permission can be revoked any time from Android Settings.

2. Camera

Tools
QR Generator/Scanner, Document Scanner, Magnifier, Camera Inspector, Image OCR, Live Color Picker.
Accessed
Camera frames in real time.
Used for
On-device processing using Google's ML Kit (the on-device variant: no Firebase ML, no Google Cloud Vision). Frames are analyzed in memory for barcodes, text, document edges, or color.
Goes to
Nowhere. No frames are saved. No frames are uploaded.

3. Microphone

Tools
Decibel Meter.
Accessed
Real-time audio amplitude (RMS) only.
Used for
Displaying the current sound level. The audio buffer is read, the loudness measured, and discarded.
Goes to
Nowhere. No audio is recorded or transmitted.

4. Contacts and call history

Tools
Call Log / Contacts tool.
Accessed
Names, phone numbers, and call entries (incoming/outgoing/missed, date, duration) via Android's standard Contacts and Call Log providers.
Used for
Displaying them on screen so you can browse your contacts and view a 7-day call summary.
Goes to
Nowhere. Nothing is persisted to disk or transmitted. Closing the tool clears the in-memory list.

5. Notifications

Tool
Notification History.
Accessed
When (and only when) you enable Notification History and grant the Notification Listener permission, Poof receives a copy of every notification posted to your device, including the title, body text, and posting app.
Used for
Storing the captured notifications in an on-device database so you can search and review them later.
Goes to
Nowhere we control. Stored locally on your device.

You can:

  • Disable capture entirely from the tool's settings.
  • Exclude specific apps from capture.
  • Skip ongoing/persistent notifications.
  • Suppress text capture (keep titles only).
  • Auto-prune entries older than your chosen retention period.
  • Manually delete individual or all entries.
  • Export entries as a CSV (Pro feature), saved to your Downloads folder or shared via Android's share sheet to a destination you pick. Poof does not upload it anywhere on its own.

Notifications can contain sensitive information (one-time codes, message previews, banking alerts). Capture is opt-in and you control what is captured.

6. Installed apps and usage stats

Tool
App Inspector.
Accessed
The list of apps installed on your device, their permissions, install/update times, sizes, target SDK, and (if you grant Usage Access) their last-used dates.
Used for
Displaying the list so you can audit what's installed and what permissions each app declares.
Goes to
Nowhere. The list is never transmitted. You can optionally export visible apps as a CSV (Pro feature) saved to your Downloads folder.

7. Files and media you select

Tools
PDF tools (merge, split, compress, watermark, sign, redact, etc.), Image tools (compress, convert, redact, resize, watermark, EXIF viewer, etc.), OCR, Document Scanner.
Accessed
Only the specific files you pick using Android's system file picker. Poof does not browse your storage.
Used for
Processing on-device using local libraries (PDFBox, Android image APIs, on-device ML Kit). Outputs are saved to a destination you choose.
Goes to
Nowhere. No file is uploaded. Temporary working files in the app's cache are deleted when the operation finishes.

8. Sensors

Tools
Compass (rotation vector), Qibla (accelerometer + magnetometer), Bubble Level (accelerometer).
Accessed
Real-time sensor readings via Android's SensorManager.
Used for
Calculating orientation, heading, or tilt for live display.
Goes to
Nowhere. Readings are not stored or transmitted.

9. Clipboard

Tools
Several tools (Base64 encoder, base converter, unit converter, NFC writer, coordinate paste, etc.) include a "Paste from clipboard" button. The Universal Inspector also reads the clipboard once on app launch to detect shared text and route it to the right tool.
Used for
Showing the pasted content in the relevant tool's input field so you can act on it.
Goes to
Nowhere. Clipboard contents are not persisted unless you explicitly save them inside a tool (e.g. saving a generated QR code to QR History).

What leaves your device

Two tools, and only when you use them.

Speed Test → Cloudflare

When you run a speed test, the tool exchanges data with Cloudflare's public speed-test infrastructure:

  • https://speed.cloudflare.com/meta : returns your public IP, ISP name, and detected city
  • https://speed.cloudflare.com/__down : downloads test bytes for download-speed measurement
  • https://speed.cloudflare.com/__up : uploads random bytes for upload-speed measurement

Test results (Mbps, latency, jitter) are saved to a local on-device database so you can see your history. Nothing about your test results is sent to Poof or anywhere else. Cloudflare's privacy policy applies to the Cloudflare side: cloudflare.com/privacypolicy

Currency Converter → open.er-api.com

When you open the Currency Converter, it fetches current exchange rates from https://open.er-api.com/v6/latest/{currency}. The request contains only the base-currency code (e.g. USD). Rates are cached on your device so the converter works offline.

No personally identifying information is sent to either service. Both requests are made from your device's IP (same as any web browser) and contain no Poof-assigned user ID or device identifier (because none exists).

What we do not do

  • No analytics. No Firebase, Mixpanel, Amplitude, Segment, Sentry, Bugsnag, Crashlytics, AppsFlyer, Adjust, or anything similar.
  • No advertising. No AdMob, Unity Ads, AppLovin, IronSource, etc.
  • No accounts. No sign-in, no email collection, no profile.
  • No device identifiers. No GAID, ANDROID_ID, IMEI, MAC address.
  • No cloud backup. Auto-backup and device-transfer rules are disabled, so your Poof data does not go to Google Drive even if the rest of your phone does.
  • No data selling. There is no data to sell. There is no buyer.

Pro purchases

Poof Pro is a one-time, non-recurring in-app purchase. The transaction itself is handled by Google Play. Poof receives a confirmation that you own the unlock and stores a single boolean (is_pro = true) in app-private storage. We don't see your name, email, payment method, or transaction ID. Google Play's privacy policy governs the purchase: policies.google.com/privacy

Children

Poof is a general-purpose utility app. It is not directed at children. The app does not knowingly collect any data that could identify a child or anyone else.

Your controls

  • Revoke any permission from Android Settings → Apps → Poof → Permissions.
  • Delete any captured data from inside the relevant tool (notification history, QR history, speed-test history, etc.).
  • Clear all app data from Android Settings → Apps → Poof → Storage → Clear data. This wipes every preference, history entry, and cached file. Poof will be in its first-launch state.
  • Uninstall. Removing the app removes all of its data (Auto-Backup is disabled, so nothing is restored elsewhere).

Changes to this policy

If we change what the app does with data, this policy will be updated and the effective date at the top will change. Material changes will be highlighted in-app on first launch after the change.
